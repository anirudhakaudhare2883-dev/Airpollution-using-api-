Abstract & Hypothesis

Urban air pollution remains one of the most critical public health hazards of the 21st century. Traditional monitoring stations are expensive, sparse, and entirely reactive—reporting pollution only after it has accumulated.

The Hypothesis: By aggregating historical meteorological data (temperature, humidity, pressure) alongside specific spatial pollutant concentrations (PM2.5, PM10, NO2), we hypothesize that non-linear machine learning ensembles can map complex environmental interactions to predict the Air Quality Index (AQI) of a given coordinate with high degrees of accuracy, enabling proactive rather than reactive public health measures.

Research Methodology

The project abandons simple linear regression in favor of the Random Forest Regressor algorithm. Environmental factors exhibit high collinearity and non-linear boundaries (e.g., sudden temperature drops causing thermal inversions that trap PM2.5).

A Random Forest ensemble constructs thousands of independent decision trees during training. By aggregating their predictions, the system becomes highly resistant to overfitting on isolated anomalous weather events.

The rigorous evaluation procedure utilizes k-fold cross-validation. Performance is governed strictly by the Coefficient of Determination (R² Score) to measure variance explained, alongside Mean Absolute Error (MAE) and Root Mean Squared Error (RMSE) to penalize vast deviations.

Societal Impact & Future Scope

Immediate Impact: Citizens receive localized, real-time insights, allowing sensitive individuals (asthma, COPD patients) to alter their outdoor schedules based on predictive analytics rather than outdated historical averages.

Future Scope: The architecture is designed to scale horizontally. Future iterations will support direct API integrations for IoT smart-home devices (automatically triggering indoor air purifiers when outdoor AQI is predicted to spike), and provide predictive macro-trends for city planners to dynamically route traffic away from impending smog zones.
